Shoes are uniquely good at wrecking a small space. They pile up right where you walk in, they sprawl across the closet floor, and a family's worth of them can bury an entryway in a week. The problem usually isn't *how many* shoes you own. It's that they have no assigned home, so they colonize the floor by default. The fix is to give them a defined spot, ideally vertical or hidden, sized to what you actually wear. Here's how to store shoes in a small apartment, whether your problem is the entryway, the closet, or both.

**The short version:** get shoes off the floor and out of the walkway. An over-the-door organizer or a hanging rack uses the back of a door; a slim shoe cabinet or a storage bench hides them behind a front; stackable racks and under-bed boxes handle the closet. Keep the current season by the door and store the rest. Honestly, thinning the collection does more than any organizer.

## The one rule: go vertical or hidden {#principle}

Shoes on the floor read as clutter, full stop. A visible pile is what makes an entryway or closet feel chaotic even when nothing else is out of place. So the governing idea of small-space shoe storage is simple: get them off the floor and out of sight. That means using vertical real estate (the back of a door, the height of a wall) or hiding them behind a front (a cabinet, a bench with a lid). Every solution below is a version of that one idea. Give the shoes a home and the space around them settles down fast.

## Use the back of the door {#door}

The back of your entryway or closet door is storage doing nothing, and it's the fastest shoe fix there is. An over-the-door organizer with pockets hangs in seconds and holds a dozen or so pairs on space you weren't using, with no floor cost and no tools. It's the same trick from our [over-the-door organizer guide](/guides/best-over-the-door-organizers-small-apartments/), pointed at footwear. A hanging shoe rack on the closet rod does the same for a closet. One caveat worth knowing: the fabric pockets suit flats, sneakers, and sandals better than tall boots or heavy work boots, which can sag them out. For everyday shoes, though, this is the highest-return, lowest-effort move you can make, and it comes down clean when you move.

## Slim cabinets &amp; benches {#cabinet}

When you want shoes *hidden* rather than just off the floor, a slim shoe cabinet is the answer. These are shallow units, often ten to fifteen inches deep, with tilt-out drawers that hold shoes on edge, so a collection tucks behind a clean front along an entryway wall. Worth knowing before you buy: the tilt-out design holds low shoes well but usually won't fit tall boots, and capacity is smaller than the sleek photos suggest, so measure and count. A shoe storage bench does double duty, since you sit on it to put shoes on while they're stored inside, retiring both a rack and a hall chair. Either one keeps the entryway looking tidy, which matters most in the first spot you see coming home.

## Closet &amp; under-bed storage {#closet}

For the shoes that aren't in daily rotation, the closet and the space under your bed do the heavy lifting. A stackable shoe rack turns the closet floor's wasted vertical air into two or three tiers, and clear stackable boxes protect pairs while stacking up a closet wall. Under the bed, a low shoe organizer or a drawer on wheels swallows off-season and special-occasion pairs, the same under-bed real estate we cover in our [under-bed storage guide](/guides/best-under-bed-storage-containers/). Just measure your clearance first, because low bed frames and platform beds may not leave room for much. Send the shoes you rarely wear to these spots so the entryway only holds what you're actually using this week.

## Curate before you store {#curate}

Before buying a single organizer, do the free step: pull every pair and be honest about what you wear. Most of us keep shoes we haven't touched in a year, and no storage system beats owning fewer. Keep the current season's rotation accessible by the door, box or store the off-season pairs, and let go of the ones you never wear. Curating first often shows that the entryway wasn't too small, it was just holding a few times the shoes it needed to. Then size your storage to the pairs that are left, not the pile you started with.

## Common shoe-storage mistakes {#mistakes}

Three to avoid. **Leaving shoes on the floor**, since a visible pile is the whole problem, so get them vertical or hidden. **Storing every pair by the door**, when you should keep only the current season in the entryway and send the rest to the closet or under the bed, or any rack overflows in days. And **buying storage before curating**, because an organizer can't fix owning more shoes than you have room for. Thin the collection first, then size the storage to what remains.

## Common questions {#faq}

### How do I store shoes in a small apartment?

Get them off the floor and out of sight: hang an over-the-door organizer to use the back of a door, add a slim shoe cabinet or storage bench to hide them behind a front along the entryway, and send off-season pairs to stackable closet racks or under-bed boxes. Keep only the current season's shoes by the door and store the rest, so the entryway holds what you actually wear.

### What's the best shoe storage for a small entryway?

For most entryways, a slim shoe cabinet (shallow, with tilt-out drawers) hides the collection behind a clean front while taking up little depth, or a storage bench lets you sit to put shoes on with them stored inside. If floor space is truly gone, an over-the-door organizer clears the pile using space you weren't using at all. Match it to whether you want the shoes hidden or just off the floor, and to whether your shoes are mostly low or include tall boots.

### How can I store shoes without taking up floor space?

Go vertical and hidden. The back of a door holds a dozen or so pairs in an over-the-door organizer; a hanging rack uses the closet rod; stackable racks climb the closet floor's vertical air; and under-bed boxes swallow off-season pairs. Combining a door organizer for daily shoes with under-bed storage for the rest keeps most of your footwear off the floor.
