The single most wasted space in almost every small bedroom is the rectangle of air under the bed. Filled well, it can hold an entire season's clothes, spare bedding, or shoes — clearing a dresser or closet's worth of stuff. The trick is choosing the right container for your bed height and what you're storing. Here's how.

## Measure first {#measure}

Before buying anything, measure the **clearance** from the floor to the bottom of your bed frame. This one number decides everything — a container an inch too tall simply won't slide under. Standard platform beds give you 6–11 inches; beds on risers give much more. Also measure the depth you can reach, since a container you can't pull out fully is a container you won't use.

## The four types {#types}

**Rolling drawers with wheels.** The gold standard. Wheels turn a heavy, dust-collecting bin into something you glide out with one hand. Best for clothes and shoes you access regularly.

**Zippered fabric bags.** Soft-sided and collapsible, ideal for bulky, infrequently used items like comforters and winter coats. They squish to fit and store flat when empty, but offer less protection from crushing.

**Clear plastic totes with lids.** The see-through option, so you know what's inside without opening. Look for low-profile "under-bed" specific totes; standard totes are usually too tall.

**Vacuum-seal bags.** Not a container exactly, but paired with any bin they shrink puffy bedding to a fraction of its size — the highest space-savings per dollar for anything soft.

## If your bed is very low {#lowbeds}

If you've got only a few inches of clearance, you have two moves: use flat, low-profile fabric organizers designed for tight gaps, or add **bed risers** to lift the frame 3–8 inches and unlock the whole zone. Risers are cheap, install in minutes, and instantly multiply your storage — just don't exceed their weight rating.
