The ugly countertop is the thing you can't unsee in a rental kitchen: scratched laminate, a dated speckled pattern, a color nobody has chosen on purpose since 1994. You can't replace it — it's not yours, and it's bolted down — and a landlord isn't resurfacing a counter for a tenant. Peel-and-stick countertop film is the fix almost nobody knows about: it's a thick, waterproof adhesive wrap, usually in a marble, granite, or wood print, that you squeegee down over the existing counter to hide it completely, and peel off when you leave. It isn't stone and it won't last forever, but for a rental you'll be in a year or three it's transformative. The trick is buying the thick, waterproof kind and prepping the surface so it lasts. ## How to choose countertop film {#choose}

Two things decide whether countertop film looks like real stone or peels up at the sink edge within a month, and the first is what you buy.

**Buy thick and waterproof — thin contact paper fails on a counter.** A countertop takes abuse a wall never does: water, heat, scrubbing, and the constant friction of plates and pans. Thin, cheap shelf-liner contact paper tears, scratches through to the ugly counter underneath, and lets water creep under the edges until it lifts. What you want is a film made *for countertops*: thick, waterproof, with a protective top coat that resists scratches and wipes clean. Matte finishes hide seams and the texture of the counter beneath better than high-gloss, which can look plasticky and shows every imperfection under kitchen lighting. Pay a little more for a thick waterproof film and it reads as real stone and survives daily cooking; buy the thin stuff and you'll be re-doing it — or looking at peeled corners — within weeks. The same thickness-and-waterproofing rule separates the lasting from the disposable in our [peel-and-stick backsplash tiles](/guides/best-peel-and-stick-backsplash-tiles-renters/) and [floor tiles](/guides/best-peel-and-stick-floor-tiles-renters/) too.

**Prep the counter and seal the edges — that's where it lives or dies.** The install is simple but unforgiving of shortcuts. Clean the counter with a degreaser and let it dry completely — any grease or dust stops the adhesive from bonding. Measure and cut with a few inches of overhang, then peel the backing gradually and squeegee the film down from the center outward to push every air bubble to the edge; a bubble left in the middle becomes a permanent lump. The failure point is always the edges, especially around the sink: wrap the film over and under the counter's front lip, trim tight to the sink and backsplash, and consider a bead of clear silicone caulk along the sink and backsplash seams so water can't get underneath. Do the prep and seal the edges and a good film lasts for years; skip them and water finds the seams fast. ## Common questions {#faq}

### Does peel-and-stick countertop film hold up to water and heat?

A film made for countertops does, within limits. Buy the thick, waterproof kind with a protective top coat — not thin shelf-liner contact paper — and it shrugs off water, scrubbing, and daily cooking as long as you seal the edges around the sink so water can't get underneath. Heat is the one caution: it's a vinyl film, so never set a hot pan directly on it. Use trivets and cutting boards exactly as you would on real laminate or even stone, and a good film lasts for years.

### Will countertop film peel off cleanly when I move out?

Yes, that's the whole point for renters. Quality countertop film is removable — warm it with a hair dryer to soften the adhesive and peel it slowly, and it lifts off the laminate underneath without residue, so the counter looks exactly as it did before and your deposit is safe. Any slight adhesive residue wipes away with a little rubbing alcohol or an adhesive remover. Because the film is protecting the original counter the whole time, it often comes back looking better than when you started.

### How do I keep the edges from peeling up?

Edges are where countertop film fails, so give them attention. Wrap the film over and under the counter's front lip rather than stopping at the edge, trim it tight against the sink and backsplash, and squeegee firmly so the adhesive fully bonds. The high-wear zones — the front edge and around the sink — benefit from a thin bead of clear silicone caulk along the seam, which stops water from working underneath. Clean, dry prep before you start matters just as much: any grease or dust and the edges never bond in the first place.

### How much countertop film do I need?

Measure the length and depth of every counter run, then add several inches to the depth so you can wrap the film over the front lip and a bit underneath. Add extra length for each seam and for wrapping around the sink cutout. Buy a little more than the bare measurement suggests — you'll want overhang to trim cleanly, and a spare piece for mistakes or future patching. Most counters take one or two rolls; an L-shaped kitchen with an island may need three, so measure each section and total them before ordering.
