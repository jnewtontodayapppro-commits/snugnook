In most small apartments the laundry lives in a closet or a corner, and the machines eat all of it — except the wall above them, which sits empty all the way to the ceiling. That vertical strip is the best storage you own and the one you never use. Filling it means detergent, softener, spare towels, and the stray-sock basket land within arm's reach instead of on the floor or in another room. The trick is choosing a shelf that clears your machine lids and, if you have a front-loader, one that also gives you somewhere to hang the clothes that can't go in the dryer. Here's how the styles compare and the specific units worth buying.

**For most small laundry spaces, a freestanding over-the-machine shelving tower is the best pick — it straddles the washer and dryer, needs no drilling, and turns the empty wall above them into three or four shelves of storage.** If you have a front-loader, an expandable shelf with a built-in hanging rod adds a place for line-dry clothes; if the gap beside the machines is the only slack, a slim rolling cart fills it. Below, the best pick for each situation and the height to measure first.

## How to choose over-machine storage {#choose}

Three measurements decide whether an over-the-machine unit fits, and most returns come from skipping one of them.

**Clearance over the lid.** This is the number people forget. A top-loading washer needs its lid to swing all the way up, so the lowest shelf has to sit above the fully-open lid — not above the closed machine. Measure from the floor to the top of the raised lid, then add a few inches of hand room, and check that the unit's bottom shelf clears it. Front-loaders don't have this problem, which is why they pair so well with a low shelf or a hanging rod right above the door.

**Freestanding or wall-mounted.** A freestanding tower has legs that straddle the machines and rest on the floor beside them, so it holds real weight and needs no holes — the right call for a rental or a heavy detergent load. A wall-mounted or over-the-door shelf frees the floor entirely but leans on your wall and your lease. Most small laundry closets do best with a freestanding unit sized to the gap between the machines and the ceiling.

**Width and depth of the nook.** Measure the space the machines sit in, not just the machines. A unit an inch too wide won't clear the closet walls, and one too deep will block the door. If the only slack is a narrow gap beside the washer, that's a job for a slim rolling cart, not a shelf overhead — the same one we cover in our [best laundry storage guide](/guides/best-laundry-storage-small-apartments/). Match the unit to the pocket of space you actually have.

## Common questions {#faq}

### How do I store detergent and supplies in a tiny laundry closet?

Go up. The wall above the machines is almost always empty, and a freestanding over-the-washer shelf turns it into three or four tiers for detergent, softener, towels, and baskets — no drilling, all the weight on its own legs. If the closet is too tight for a tower, a slim rolling cart in the gap beside the washer holds the essentials. Keep only what you use each wash within reach and store bulk refills elsewhere.

### Will an over-the-washer shelf fit a top-loading machine?

Only if the bottom shelf clears the fully-open lid. Measure from the floor to the top of the raised lid, add a few inches of hand room, and confirm the unit's lowest shelf sits above that — not just above the closed machine. Front-loaders skip this issue entirely, so they work with lower shelves and a hanging rod right above the door. Always measure the raised lid before buying.

### Do these need to be drilled into the wall?

Most don't. A freestanding over-the-machine tower stands on its own legs beside the washer and dryer and holds real weight with no holes, which makes it the right pick for a rental. Only wall-mounted or over-the-door shelves need hardware. If you want a hanging rod but can't drill, look for a freestanding unit that includes one rather than a wall-anchored rail.

### Where do I hang clothes that can't go in the dryer?

Add a shelf-and-rod unit above a front-loader, or a fold-down drying bar built into an over-the-machine rack. Both give line-dry clothes a home right at the machines instead of draped over a door.
