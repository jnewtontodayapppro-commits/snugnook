Wall sconces are the lighting layer renters skip, because the word "sconce" sounds like something an electrician hardwires into the wall. It doesn't have to be. Plug-in sconces mount with a couple of adhesive strips or a small removable bracket and run a cord to the nearest outlet, and cordless rechargeable ones skip even that — giving you the built-in, glowing-off-the-wall look with zero wiring and, if you choose right, zero holes. That's a big deal in a small rental, because a sconce adds light *and* frees the nightstand or shelf a lamp would occupy. The trick is picking the mount that matches your wall and your lease, and hiding the cord cleanly. Here's how the types compare and the specific sconces worth buying.

**For most renters, a plug-in wall sconce set with an adhesive or bracket mount is the best pick — it gives you a pair of matching wall lights beside the bed or sofa with no wiring, no electrician, and a cord you tuck behind furniture.** If you don't want a cord at all, a rechargeable battery sconce sticks up anywhere; a swing-arm model reaches over for reading; a picture light lifts art or a mirror; and modern or farmhouse styles let it read as decor. Below, the best pick for each situation and how to mount it damage-free.

## How to choose a renter sconce {#choose}

Three questions decide which sconce fits your wall, your lease, and where you need the light.

**Corded or cordless?** A plug-in sconce runs a cord to an outlet — brighter and never needs charging, but you'll want the outlet close and the cord hidden. A rechargeable battery sconce has no cord at all, so it goes anywhere on any wall and looks truly built-in, at the cost of recharging it every few weeks. For a spot near an outlet (beside a bed, over a sofa), plug-in is the easy call; for a bare wall far from power, cordless wins. Many renters mix both.

**How does it mount, and can you drill?** This is the renter question. The gentlest sconces mount on **adhesive strips** or a small **bracket** with two screws; adhesive leaves no holes at all, while two small anchors patch with a dab of filler at move-out. Rechargeable models often use a magnetic plate so you can lift the light off to charge it. Avoid anything that needs a junction box unless you own the place. Weight matters: a heavy metal sconce wants a bracket, while a light one is happy on adhesive — check the product's own guidance.

**Where's the light going, and how warm?** A reading sconce beside the bed wants an **adjustable swing arm** and a **2700K warm** bulb so it points at your book and glows soft; an accent sconce over a sofa can be fixed; a **picture light** aims down at art or a mirror. As with every light in a small space, warm and dimmable beats bright and flat — the same principle running through our [cozy small-space lighting guide](/guides/cozy-small-space-lighting-ideas/). ## Common questions {#faq}

### Can renters install wall sconces without an electrician?

Yes. Plug-in sconces run a cord to a standard outlet and mount with adhesive strips or a small bracket, so there's no wiring and no electrician involved. Cordless rechargeable sconces skip the cord entirely and stick to a magnetic wall plate. Both give you the look of a hardwired sconce with none of the electrical work, and both come off clean — the adhesive kind leaves no holes, and a two-screw bracket patches with a dab of filler at move-out.

### How do you hide the cord on a plug-in wall sconce?

Mount the sconce near an outlet and run the cord straight down behind the nearest furniture — a headboard, a sofa, a dresser — so it never shows. For a cleaner look, a paintable cord cover or adhesive cord clips guide it down the wall to the outlet and peel off at move-out. Positioning the sconce above and slightly to the side of an outlet keeps the run short. If a visible cord bothers you, a cordless rechargeable sconce avoids the issue entirely.

### Are plug-in or battery wall sconces better for a rental?

It depends on the wall. Plug-in sconces are brighter and never need charging, so they're best beside a bed or sofa where an outlet is close and the cord can hide. Battery (rechargeable) sconces have no cord at all, so they win on a bare wall far from power or when you want a truly built-in look — the trade is recharging them every few weeks. Many renters use plug-in where outlets are handy and cordless everywhere else.

### Where should I put wall sconces in a small bedroom?

Flanking the bed at roughly shoulder height when you're sitting up — about 24 to 30 inches above the mattress — so they light a book without glaring, and free both nightstands from lamps. A swing-arm model lets you aim the light exactly. In a small bedroom that's the highest-value spot, since it adds a warm layer and reclaims the nightstand surface at once. Over a dresser or beside a mirror are good secondary spots.
