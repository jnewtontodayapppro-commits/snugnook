The bed takes up half a small bedroom and, in most rooms, gives nothing back — it's a mattress on legs over a shin-high cave of dead air. A storage bed fixes that by turning the space under the mattress into real storage: drawers in the frame, or a base that lifts on gas struts to reveal the whole footprint underneath. In a tight room that's not a nice-to-have, it's often the single piece that decides whether you need a dresser at all. The catch is that "storage bed" covers a few very different designs, and the right one depends on your room, your budget, and whether you'd rather pull a drawer or lift the whole mattress. Here's how they compare, and the specific frames worth buying.

**For most small bedrooms, a platform bed with built-in drawers is the best storage bed — it replaces a dresser in the bed's own footprint.** If you want maximum storage, a lift-up (ottoman) base opens the entire area under the mattress; if you're on a budget, a metal frame with tall clearance lets you slide bins under for a fraction of the price. Below, the best pick for each situation.

## The types, and which fits a small room {#types}

There are three real designs, and they store differently.

**Drawer platform beds** have two to four drawers built into the sides of the frame. They're the sweet spot for most small bedrooms: no risers, no loose bins, and four drawers can genuinely stand in for a chest of drawers. The one thing to check is which side the drawers open — you need clear floor beside the bed to pull them, so on a bed pushed tight into a corner, confirm the drawers come out the *foot* or the open side, not the wall side.

**Lift-up (ottoman) beds** hydraulically lift the entire mattress and base on gas struts, exposing the whole footprint underneath — far more volume than drawers, and perfect for bulky, seldom-touched things (out-of-season bedding, luggage, the winter duvet). The trade-off: you clear the bed to get in, so it's for storage you access monthly, not daily. It needs zero extra floor to open, which makes it ideal when the bed is boxed into a corner.

**Storage-headboard beds** add cubbies or a shelf behind your head — a bookshelf-headboard that replaces the nightstand you may not have room for. Often combined with drawers below. Great when the bedside gap is too tight for any nightstand at all.

Whichever you choose, place it right first — where the bed goes and which way the drawers open is a layout decision, covered in our [small bedroom layout guide](/guides/small-bedroom-layout-ideas/). And a storage bed pairs naturally with the rest of a tight-room plan in our [small bedroom storage ideas](/guides/small-bedroom-storage-ideas/).

## Common questions {#faq}

### Are storage beds good for small bedrooms?

They're one of the best buys you can make. A storage bed turns the dead space under the mattress into a dresser's worth of storage without adding any floor, which is exactly the trade a small bedroom needs. A four-drawer platform bed can let you skip a dresser entirely; a lift-up base stores even more for things you don't reach daily.

### Drawer bed or lift-up (ottoman) bed — which is better for a small room?

Drawers for daily-access storage, lift-up for maximum volume. Drawer platform beds let you grab clothes without clearing the bed, but you need clear floor beside the bed to open them. Lift-up beds hold far more and need no extra floor to open (great in a corner), but you clear the mattress to get in, so they suit bulky, seldom-touched items like spare bedding and luggage.

### Do you need bed risers with a storage bed?

No — that's the point. A storage bed builds the storage into the frame (drawers) or the base (lift-up), so you skip risers and loose bins entirely. Risers plus under-bed bins are the cheaper retrofit for a bed you already own; a storage bed is the built-in version.
