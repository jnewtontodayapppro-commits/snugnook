Working from home in a small apartment means the office has to share space with the rest of your life — and ideally disappear when you're off the clock. The goal isn't a big desk; it's a workspace that's ergonomic while you use it and invisible when you don't. Here are the four setups that work, and the gear that makes each one.

## The corner desk {#corner}

An empty corner is the most common home for a small-space desk. A compact **L-shaped or corner desk** uses the two walls to give you more usable surface than its footprint suggests, and leaves the center of the room open. Add a wall shelf above it and you've got vertical storage without eating floor space.

## The fold-away wall desk {#foldaway}

For a true studio where the desk can't live out permanently, a **wall-mounted fold-down desk** is the answer. It drops from the wall to a full work surface, then folds flat (often with your laptop and papers still tucked inside) when you're done. Your living room stops being an office at 5 p.m.

## The closet office {#cloffice}

If you have a closet you can spare, convert it: a shelf at desk height becomes the work surface, the shelf above holds supplies, and the doors close on the whole thing at the end of the day. It's the ultimate "hide the office" move and needs no permanent changes if you use tension rods and freestanding shelving.

## Ergonomics in a small footprint {#ergo}

Small doesn't have to mean uncomfortable. A **laptop stand** raises your screen to eye level (paired with a compact external keyboard) to save your neck, and a **monitor riser** with storage underneath reclaims desk space. Even in a tight setup, getting the screen up and the keyboard down is the difference between a workspace you can use all day and one that leaves you aching.
