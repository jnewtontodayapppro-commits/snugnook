A floor lamp is the most space-efficient light you can buy, and most small apartments own too few of them. It stands in a corner you weren't using, throws warm light up and across the room, and — unlike a table lamp — needs no table, shelf, or nightstand to sit on. In a small space where every surface is spoken for, that last part matters: a floor lamp adds a whole layer of light without costing you an inch of usable surface. The trick is matching the *kind* of floor lamp to the job — an arc to reach over a sofa, a torchiere to bounce light off the ceiling, a shelf lamp to double as a side table, a corner lamp for mood. Here's how the types compare and the specific lamps worth buying.

**For most small living rooms, an arc floor lamp is the best pick — it curves out over the sofa or a chair to drop light right where you sit, without needing a side table under it.** If floor space is tight, a lamp with built-in shelves doubles as a side table; a corner LED lamp fills a dead corner with mood light; a slim reading lamp handles a chair; and a torchiere bounces soft light off the ceiling. Below, the best pick for each situation and the two specs to check first.

## How to choose a floor lamp {#choose}

Two numbers and one decision sort this out. The numbers are on the box; the decision is about your corner.

**Footprint is the whole point — protect it.** A floor lamp's base is its entire cost in floor space, usually under a square foot, and a slim one tucks into a corner that was doing nothing. Compare that to a table lamp, which needs a whole surface you probably don't have to spare. So the small-space move is to read light off the floor and the walls, not the tabletops. If you're torn between two lamps, the one with the smaller base or the one that adds a surface (shelves) wins — it either takes less or gives more.

**Match the light shape to the job.** An **arc** reaches horizontally out over seating, so it lights a reading spot with no table beneath. A **torchiere** points up and washes the ceiling, which lifts the whole room softly and reads as bigger — one of the [tricks that make a small room look bigger](/guides/make-small-room-look-bigger/). A **multi-head** lamp aims several small pools from one base — good when you want to light a couple of zones without a couple of lamps. A **reading lamp** is a focused, adjustable task light for a chair or desk. A **corner LED** lamp is pure ambience for a dead corner. Decide what the corner needs before you shop the look.

**Get the bulb warm and dimmable.** The lamp is only as cozy as the light in it: aim for **2700K "soft white"** bulbs (or a lamp with selectable color temperature) and a way to dim, so the corner glows in the evening instead of glaring. This is the same layering logic behind our whole [cozy small-space lighting guide](/guides/cozy-small-space-lighting-ideas/) — a floor lamp is the low-and-warm anchor layer. Pair it with wall light from our [plug-in wall sconces roundup](/guides/best-plug-in-wall-sconces-renters/) and you've got two layers with zero surface cost.

## Common questions {#faq}

### Are floor lamps good for small spaces?

Yes — they're one of the most space-efficient lights you can buy. A floor lamp adds a whole layer of light from a corner you weren't using, and because it stands on the floor it costs you none of the table or shelf space a table lamp would need. In a small apartment where surfaces are scarce, that makes a floor lamp the easy way to light a room without cluttering it. Choose a slim base or a model with built-in shelves to save or add space.

### What kind of floor lamp is best for a small living room?

An arc floor lamp for most people — it reaches out over the sofa to light a reading spot without a side table underneath. If you need a surface too, a lamp with built-in shelves doubles as an end table; if you just want ambience, a corner LED lamp or a torchiere fills a dead corner with soft light. Match the lamp's light shape to what your specific corner needs rather than buying on looks alone.

### How tall should a floor lamp be?

Most floor lamps run 58 to 72 inches, and that range works for a small room. For reading, you want the bottom of the shade roughly at eye level when you're seated so the bulb doesn't glare — around 60 to 65 inches. A torchiere or uplight can be taller since it aims at the ceiling. In a small space, a slim, taller lamp often reads better than a short, wide one because it draws the eye up.

### What bulb should I use in a floor lamp for a cozy room?

A 2700K "soft white" bulb, dimmable if you can. That warm, slightly golden tone is what makes a corner feel cozy rather than clinical — skip 5000K "daylight" bulbs anywhere you relax. If the lamp has selectable color temperature, keep it on the warm setting in the evening. Getting the bulb right matters as much as the lamp itself, and it's nearly free to fix.
