On a small desk, the smartest upgrade isn't a bigger desk — it's getting the screen up to eye level and clearing the surface underneath it. A laptop sitting flat on the desk drags your head down and your shoulders forward, and it hogs the very surface you're short on. Raise the screen and you fix the small-desk hunch that leaves your neck aching by noon; do it with a riser that stores things underneath, an arm that clamps to the edge, or a stand that holds the laptop upright, and you get desk space back at the same time. Screen up, desk clear: on a tiny workspace that combination is worth more than the extra square footage you don't have. Here's how the options compare and the specific stands and risers worth buying.

**For most small desks, a monitor riser with built-in storage is the best pick — it lifts the screen to eye level to fix the hunch and reclaims the space beneath it for a keyboard, stationery, or clutter, all in one piece.** If you work on a laptop, an adjustable laptop stand does the lifting; a standing converter adds a sit-stand option; a monitor arm frees the entire desktop; a vertical stand parks the laptop upright; and an under-desk keyboard tray clears the surface completely. Below, the best pick for each setup and how to get the height right.

## How to choose a stand or riser {#choose}

Two goals drive every choice here — get the screen to eye level, and win back desk surface — and how you balance them depends on your setup.

**Get the height right: the top of the screen at eye level.** The ergonomic target is simple — when you sit up straight, the top of your screen should sit at or just below eye level, so your gaze drops slightly rather than your neck bending down. Measure the gap from your desk to your eye line and pick a riser or stand that covers it; most people need three to six inches of lift for a monitor, more for a laptop. Pair any laptop lift with an external keyboard and mouse, because once the laptop is raised you can't type on it comfortably — screen up, hands down is the whole point, and it's the fix our [tiny-apartment home office guide](/guides/home-office-tiny-apartment/) leans on hardest.

**Win back the surface: store under it, clamp it, or stand it up.** On a small desk every square inch counts, so favor the option that gives surface back. A riser with a drawer or shelf turns the space under the screen into storage; a monitor arm clamps to the desk edge and floats the screen with nothing underneath at all; a vertical stand holds a closed laptop on its edge like a book, taking a fraction of the footprint; and an under-desk keyboard tray moves the keyboard off the desktop entirely. Decide whether you'd rather store things under the screen or have nothing there — that's the main fork in the road.

**Match it to laptop, monitor, or both.** A laptop-only setup wants a laptop stand (in use) plus maybe a vertical stand (when closed); a monitor setup wants a riser or an arm; a sit-stand worker wants a converter that lifts the whole work surface. Consider how permanent it is, too: a clamp-on arm or tray needs a desk edge it can grip and suits a setup that stays put, while a riser or stand just sits on top and moves with you — the renter-friendly, no-install choice. Clearing the clutter that gathers around all of this is the job of our [desk organizers roundup](/guides/best-desk-organizers-small-spaces/); this guide is about height and surface.

## Common questions {#faq}

### How high should a monitor or laptop stand raise my screen?

High enough that the top of the screen sits at or just below your eye level when you're sitting up straight, so your gaze drops slightly instead of your neck bending down. Most people need about three to six inches of lift for a monitor and more for a laptop. Measure the gap between your current screen top and your eye line, and pick a riser or stand that covers it. Always pair a raised laptop with an external keyboard, since you can't type comfortably once the laptop is lifted.

### Do monitor stands actually save desk space?

The right ones do. A riser with a drawer or shelf turns the dead space under the screen into storage; a clamp-on monitor arm removes the base from the desktop entirely; a vertical stand parks a laptop on its edge in a fraction of the footprint; and an under-desk keyboard tray moves the keyboard off the surface. A plain riser mainly buys you height, but any of these designed with storage or clamping in mind genuinely reclaims surface — which on a small desk is the whole point.

### Monitor arm or monitor riser — which is better for a small desk?

A riser is simpler and cheaper, sits on the desk with no install, moves with you, and gives you storage underneath — best for renters and laptop-plus-monitor setups. A monitor arm clamps to the desk edge and frees the entire desktop, with the most flexible positioning, but needs a compatible desk edge and a VESA-mount monitor. Choose the riser if you want storage and zero install; choose the arm if maximum clear desk surface and adjustability matter most.

### Is a standing desk converter worth it in a small space?

If you want to stand part of the day without room for a full standing desk, yes. A compact converter sits on your existing desk and lifts the screen and keyboard up on demand, then lowers back to sit — giving you both postures from one small desk. The trade-offs are that it takes up the desktop when lowered and adds some height, so measure your desk and pick a compact model. For a small home office where a second standing desk is out of the question, it's the practical way to get the sit-stand benefit.
