A small desk doesn't stay clear for long. The monitor eats the back third, the keyboard and mouse own the front, and everything else — pens, chargers, the notebook, the coffee — fights for the strip in between. The fix isn't a bigger desk. It's a handful of organizers that reclaim the surface you already have: lift the monitor and store things under it, float the screen off the desk entirely, get the cords off the floor, and push what's left up onto the wall. Buy the two or three that match your actual mess, not a matching set of ten. Here's what each one solves.

**For most small desks, start with a monitor riser that has storage underneath — it fixes the two biggest problems at once, raising your screen to eye level and reclaiming the desk space beneath it.** Add a no-drill cable tray to clear the cords, and a wall pegboard to move pens and supplies off the surface entirely. Below, the best pick in each category and the problem it solves.

## How to choose desk organizers {#choose}

Don't buy a set — buy the fix for your specific problem. Look at your desk and diagnose it.

**Monitor eating your surface?** The space under a monitor is dead air, and the space in front of it is where your neck slumps. A **riser with a drawer** solves both: it lifts the screen toward eye level and turns the gap underneath into storage for the keyboard, a notebook, or supplies. If you want the whole desktop back, a **monitor arm** clamps to the edge and floats the screen off the surface completely.

**Cords everywhere?** A tangle of cables under and behind a small desk collects dust and makes the whole setup read as chaos. An **under-desk cable tray** gathers the power strip and cords into one hidden channel — and the no-drill, clamp-on kind keeps a rental's desk and walls unmarked.

**Surface still cluttered?** When the desk itself is full, the wall above it is empty. A **pegboard** puts pens, headphones, cables, and small supplies up on the vertical plane where they're visible and off the surface — the same go-vertical logic that runs through our [vertical storage ideas](/guides/vertical-storage-ideas-small-apartments/).

**Desk has no drawer?** Plenty of compact desks skip storage to stay slim. A **clip-on or slide-out under-desk drawer** adds a hidden home for pens and clutter without adding any footprint. It's the cheapest way to make a bare writing desk functional. This whole setup pairs with picking the right desk in the first place — see our [best small desks for small spaces](/guides/best-small-desks-small-spaces/) roundup.

## Common questions {#faq}

### What's the best desk organizer for a small desk?

A monitor riser with a built-in drawer, hands down. It raises your screen to a healthier height and turns the dead space underneath into storage, fixing ergonomics and clutter in one piece. If your desk is small enough that the monitor's base is the issue, a clamp-on monitor arm that floats the screen off the surface is the next step.

### How do I hide cables under a small desk?

Clamp a no-drill cable management tray under the desktop and route the power strip and cords into it, off the floor. Add a few adhesive clips along the desk leg to guide cables down cleanly. The no-drill, clamp-on kind is best for renters since it leaves no marks. Getting cords off the floor also makes cleaning far easier.

### How do I organize a desk with no drawers?

Add the storage the desk lacks. A clip-on or slide-out under-desk drawer gives pens and clutter a hidden home without any extra footprint, and a wall pegboard above the desk holds supplies vertically. Between the two you get a full workstation's worth of storage on a bare writing desk. Keep only what you use daily on the surface itself.

### Is a monitor arm worth it on a small desk?

On a genuinely small desk, often yes — the monitor's base can eat a surprising amount of surface, and floating the screen gives all of it back plus room to push the keyboard around freely. It also lets you position the screen at the exact height and distance your neck wants. If your desk is roomier or your budget is tight, a riser with storage delivers most of the benefit for less.
