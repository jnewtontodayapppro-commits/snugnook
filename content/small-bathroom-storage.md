Small rental bathrooms are a special kind of challenge: often just a sink, a toilet, and zero storage, with a lease that forbids drilling. The fix is a stack of damage-free solutions that add real storage to the walls, the door, and the awkward space over the toilet — and lift away cleanly when you leave. Here's the plan.

## Claim the space over the toilet {#overtoilet}

The wall above the toilet is the biggest chunk of unused space in most bathrooms. An **over-the-toilet storage rack** (the freestanding kind that straddles the tank) turns it into two or three shelves for towels, baskets, and supplies — no wall anchors required.

## Add shelves without holes {#walls}

For extra wall storage, reach for **adhesive-mounted shelves and baskets** rated for bathroom humidity, or a **tension-rod corner shelf** wedged into the shower corner. Both add real capacity with zero damage. Keep heavier items low and lighter items high for stability.

## Fix the shower chaos {#shower}

Bottles crowding the tub edge are the classic small-bathroom mess. A **hanging shower caddy** that loops over the showerhead (or a tension-rod pole caddy in the corner) gets everything up and off surfaces, drains properly, and comes down in seconds when you move.

## Rescue a tiny counter {#counter}

If you have a sliver of counter, protect it. A small **under-sink expandable organizer** works around the pipes to reclaim the cabinet or floor space below, and a compact **countertop riser shelf** doubles a narrow counter's usable area by going vertical.
