## How to choose {#how}

Three things decide whether an over-the-door organizer works or ends up in a closet:

- **Door thickness & clearance.** Measure your door's thickness and check that the hooks clear the frame. Standard hooks fit most interior doors; thicker or trimmed doors may need an over-the-top adjustable bracket or a screw-mounted option.
- **Clear pockets vs. baskets vs. hooks.** Clear pockets are best for small items you want to *see* (spices, cosmetics, craft supplies). Wire baskets suit bulkier pantry goods. Simple hook racks handle coats, bags, and towels.
- **Weight rating & door swing.** A loaded organizer adds weight and depth — make sure the door still closes and latches, especially on a closet or pantry door that sits close to a wall.

## Best for shoes and the entryway {#shoes}

An over-the-door shoe organizer with clear pockets is the classic, and it's not just for shoes. Behind a front door it corrals keys, gloves, sunscreen, and pet-walk supplies. In a bedroom closet it holds a full accessory collection. Look for reinforced pockets if you'll store heavier boots.

## Best for pantry and kitchen {#pantry}

Wire or basket-style racks mounted on the pantry (or under-sink cabinet) door turn dead space into canned-goods, spice, and wrap storage. This is often the difference between a pantry that "has no room" and one that suddenly holds a week more of food. Match the basket depth to your door clearance so the door still shuts.

## Best for bathroom and toiletries {#bath}

In a rental bathroom with no cabinet, a clear-pocket organizer on the back of the door holds an entire family's toiletries off the counter. Choose water-resistant materials and a rustproof frame, and keep the heaviest bottles in the lowest pockets for stability.
