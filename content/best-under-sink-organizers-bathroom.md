The cabinet under the bathroom sink is the single most wasted storage space in the room, and the reason is the plumbing. The P-trap and supply lines run right through the middle of it, so a flat shelf won't fit and everyone just piles bottles on the floor of the cabinet, around the pipe, where the back half is unreachable and half of what's there is forgotten. The fix isn't more cabinet — it's an organizer built to work *around* the pipe: one that adds a second level over the plumbing, slides out so you see the back, or hangs on the door where the pipe isn't. Get the geometry right and that dead, damp cabinet holds twice as much, neatly. Here's how the types compare and the specific organizer worth buying for each.

**For most bathrooms, an expandable two-tier under-sink organizer is the best pick — its legs and shelves adjust around the P-trap to add a whole second level over the plumbing, turning a cluttered cabinet floor into real shelving.** If you'd rather pull things toward you, slide-out drawer baskets bring the back row forward; stackable clear drawers and grouped bins sort the small stuff; and an over-the-door basket steals the one flat surface the pipe doesn't block. Below, the best pick for each situation and how to measure so it actually fits.

## How to choose an under-sink organizer {#choose}

Three things decide whether an under-sink organizer fits and helps, and the first is a measurement almost everyone skips.

**Measure around the pipe, not just the cabinet.** The number that kills under-sink plans is the position of the P-trap, so measure three things before you buy: the cabinet's inside width and depth, the interior height from floor to the underside of the sink, and — critically — how far the drainpipe sticks out from the back wall and how high it sits. An expandable shelf works because its legs telescope to straddle the pipe and its shelf notches around it; a fixed organizer that ignores the pipe simply won't sit flat. Sketch where the pipe lands and buy the style that clears it. Bathroom cabinets are also often shallower and shorter than kitchen ones, so don't assume a kitchen-sized organizer drops in.

**Decide how you'll reach the back.** A plain shelf doubles your levels but you still reach over the front row to get the back; a pull-out drawer basket slides the whole back row out to you like a filing drawer, which is the difference between using a deep cabinet and abandoning its back half. If your cabinet is deep, favor anything that slides. If it's shallow, a two-tier shelf plus grouped bins is plenty. The principle is the same one that runs through our [small bathroom storage guide](/guides/small-bathroom-storage-ideas-renters/): never store something where you have to move another thing to reach it.

**Buy rustproof, and group before you fill.** This cabinet gets damp — drips, humidity, the occasional leak — so choose coated steel, plastic, or bamboo that won't rust and streak, the same warning that applies to everything in a wet room. Then group before you load: put all the cleaning bottles in one bin, hair tools in another, backup toiletries in a third, so you pull one container instead of digging. Clear bins let you see what's inside; a set of matching ones turns the chaos into an inventory. ## Common questions {#faq}

### How do you organize under a bathroom sink with pipes in the way?

Use organizers built to work around the plumbing rather than ignore it. An expandable two-tier shelf has telescoping legs and a notched shelf that straddle the P-trap to add a level over it; an L-shaped pull-out wraps the corner the pipe creates; and an over-the-door basket uses the cabinet door, where there's no pipe at all. Measure how far the drainpipe sticks out and how high it sits before buying, then pick the style that clears it. The pipe is the whole puzzle — solve for it and the cabinet doubles in capacity.

### What is the best under-sink organizer for a small bathroom?

For most people, an expandable two-tier shelf, because it adjusts to fit narrow or shallow bathroom cabinets and adds a full second level over the pipe. If your cabinet is deep, add slide-out drawer baskets so the back row comes to you. Group the loose items into clear bins on top of that, and you've turned the worst-organized cabinet in the house into real, usable shelving. Just confirm the organizer's dimensions against your cabinet — bathroom cabinets run smaller than kitchen ones.

### How do I stop rust in an under-sink cabinet?

Buy rustproof materials and manage the moisture. Choose coated steel, plastic, or bamboo organizers rather than bare wire, which streaks rust in a damp cabinet within a season. Line the cabinet floor with a waterproof mat to catch drips, keep an eye on the pipe joints for slow leaks, and leave a little airflow so humidity doesn't sit. Rustproof from the start is the easy call — a cheap wire rack that rusts can stain the cabinet and cost you a deposit in a rental.

### Should I use bins or shelves under the sink?

Both, together. A shelf or pull-out solves the vertical problem — it adds levels over and around the pipe so you're not stacking bottles on the floor. Bins solve the sorting problem — they group small items by task so you grab one container instead of rummaging. The best setups use a two-tier shelf for height and a handful of clear bins on it for grouping. Shelves make the space; bins keep it from sliding back into chaos.
