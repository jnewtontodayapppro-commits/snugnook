The thing that stops people using their balcony usually isn't the size — it's the feeling of sitting on a stage. Apartment balconies look straight into the neighbor's, onto the street, or across a courtyard, and no amount of nice furniture fixes that exposed feeling. A privacy screen does, and it's the single change that turns a balcony from a place you pass through into a place you linger. The trick is picking a screen that blocks the sightline you actually mind, survives the weather, and — since most of us rent — attaches without a drill and comes off clean at move-out. Here's how the types compare and the specific screens worth buying.

**For most apartment balconies, a fabric railing privacy screen is the best pick — it zip-ties along the balustrade, blocks around 90% of the sightline, and cuts wind, all with no tools and nothing to patch on move-out.** If you want greenery, faux ivy or a natural reed roll soften the look; outdoor curtains give you a screen you can draw open; a freestanding panel needs no attachment at all; and a trellis grows you a living green wall. Below, the best pick for each situation and how to hang it damage-free.

## How to choose a privacy screen {#choose}

Three questions decide which screen fits your balcony, your lease, and the neighbors' angle.

**Which sightline are you actually blocking?** Stand where you'll sit and look for the gaze that bothers you. If it's neighbors on the same level looking across, a railing-height cover that fills the balustrade is enough. If someone looks down from above, you need height — a tall reed roll, curtains on a raised rod, or a freestanding panel that stands over railing height. Screening the whole balcony when you only needed the lower rail wastes money and boxes you in; match the coverage to the actual angle.

**Can you attach to anything, and can you drill?** Most renters can't drill, so how a screen mounts matters as much as how it looks. A fabric cover and a reed roll zip-tie or lash to the existing railing — no holes, and off clean at move-out. Curtains need a tension rod or a no-drill bracket. A freestanding panel needs nothing at all but floor space and a stable base, which makes it the pick when there's no railing to tie to or the lease forbids attachments. Check the lease too: some buildings restrict what can hang on a balcony railing at all.

**Weather, wind, and how much light you'll lose.** A solid screen that blocks 90% of the view also blocks a lot of light and catches a lot of wind, which on a high balcony can strain the railing. Perforated HDPE screens and reed rolls let some air and light through; dense faux hedge and blackout curtains block more of both. On an exposed balcony, secure a screen at multiple points and consider a more open weave. ## Common questions {#faq}

### How do I get privacy on an apartment balcony without drilling?

Use a screen that ties to what's already there or stands on its own. A fabric privacy cover and a reed or faux-ivy roll zip-tie or lash to the existing railing with no holes; outdoor curtains hang on a tension rod; and a freestanding panel needs nothing but floor space and a stable base. All of them come off clean at move-out. Check your lease first, since some buildings restrict what can hang on a balcony railing.

### What's the best balcony privacy screen for renters?

A fabric railing privacy screen is the best all-rounder — it zip-ties to the balustrade, blocks around 90% of the view, cuts wind, costs little, and leaves no trace. If you want greenery, faux ivy panels give the same no-drill install with a garden look. When you can't attach anything at all, a freestanding metal panel screens the sightline without touching the railing. Match the height to whether neighbors look across or down.

### Will a privacy screen block too much light or wind?

It can, so match the weave to your balcony. A solid 90%-blockage cover or dense faux hedge blocks the most view but also the most light and air, and on a high balcony catches serious wind. Perforated HDPE screens and natural reed rolls let some light and breeze through while still screening the view, which is the better call on an exposed, windy balcony. Secure any screen at several points so a gust can't peel it loose.

### How tall does a balcony privacy screen need to be?

It depends on the angle you're blocking. For neighbors sitting at the same level across from you, a screen that fills the railing to about hip or chest height does the job. If someone looks down from a floor above, you need real height — a tall reed roll, curtains on a raised rod, or a freestanding panel that stands well above the railing. Sit down in your usual spot and sight the exact gaze you want gone before you buy, so you get just enough height without boxing yourself in.
